GROUPYGO
============================================================

TYPE
- Own startup/product.
- Worked on it for approximately 1 year.

IMPORTANT:
- Correct spelling: GroupyGo.


------------------------------
PROBLEM STATEMENT
------------------------------

- There is a market among small and medium-sized travel agencies
  in Kerala around group/consolidated airfares.
- Different agencies/suppliers have tickets available for
  different sectors.

Examples:
- Calicut → Dubai
- Calicut → Jeddah
- London
- Delhi
- etc.

B2B COMMUNICATION PROBLEM:
- Travel agencies need to communicate available tickets
  to other B2B travel agencies.
- One of their major channels was WhatsApp.
- Agencies were sending ticket availability/pricing information
  through WhatsApp groups and broadcasts every day.
- Information was highly unorganized.
- Large amounts of ticket information could be spread across
  many WhatsApp messages/groups.
- Finding a specific ticket required manually going through
  messages and information from different sources.


------------------------------
PRODUCT IDEA
------------------------------

- Created GroupyGo to organize this process.
- Goal:
  - Make searching for group/consolidated tickets fast.
  - Organize scattered ticket information.
  - Make the existing B2B workflow easier.

Instead of initially building:
- Website
- Mobile application

I chose:
- WhatsApp as the primary interface.

WHY WHATSAPP:
- Target users were already familiar with WhatsApp.
- WhatsApp was already a major communication channel
  for the target market.
- Users did not need to learn a new platform.
- Official WhatsApp API provided useful capabilities
  for building the product.


------------------------------
MVP APPROACH
------------------------------

- Initially created an MVP.
- Did not immediately build the complete marketplace.
- Main goal was to validate:
  - Whether the problem was real.
  - Whether agencies would use the system.
  - Whether WhatsApp could work as the product interface.

Initial infrastructure:
- Admin panel.
- Team manually managed ticket/supplier data.
- Supplier dashboard and other marketplace features
  were planned for later.

The initial priority was:
- Validate the problem.
- Validate usage.
- Understand actual demand.
- Avoid building the entire product before validation.


------------------------------
HOW GROUPYGO WORKED
------------------------------

A travel agency could send a structured WhatsApp message.

Example:
CCJ DXB JAN 29

The system would:
1. Receive the WhatsApp request.
2. Process the sector/date information.
3. Search available ticket data.
4. Identify suppliers with matching tickets.
5. Return matching ticket information.

Response time:
- Approximately 2 seconds.

WhatsApp:
- Acted as the product frontend/interface.

Admin panel:
- Used to manage available ticket data.


------------------------------
VALIDATION / TRACTION
------------------------------

Pilot:
- Calicut
- Malappuram
- Malabar region

Pilot users:
- Approximately 50–60 agencies.

Later usage:
- 300+ agencies using the product.
- Approximately 300–400 searches per day.

Operation:
- Product ran for approximately one year.

This demonstrated:
- Real-world usage.
- Recurring usage.
- Actual adoption.
- Validation of the underlying problem.


------------------------------
PRODUCT DECISIONS
------------------------------

Decision 1:
- WhatsApp-first instead of website/app.

Reason:
- Existing user behavior.
- Existing communication channel.
- Familiar interface.
- Lower adoption friction.
- Official WhatsApp API capabilities.

Decision 2:
- MVP instead of complete marketplace.

Reason:
- Validate problem before building everything.

Decision 3:
- Admin-managed data initially.

Reason:
- Faster way to validate the concept.
- Supplier dashboards could be added later.


------------------------------
GROUPYGO TECH STACK
------------------------------

- Official WhatsApp API
- Node.js
- Express.js
- MongoDB
- WhatsApp as frontend/interface
- Admin panel


------------------------------
OUTCOME
------------------------------

- 300+ agencies.
- 300–400 searches/day.
- Approximately one year of operation.
- Product eventually shut down.

For portfolio purposes, the important story is:
- Clear industry problem.
- Understanding existing user behavior.
- WhatsApp-first product decision.
- MVP strategy.
- Fast validation.
- Real-world adoption.
- Product thinking.
- Efficient technical execution.

CAPMYLEAD
============================================================

TYPE
- Contract/client project.
- Travel industry client.

IMPORTANT:
- Correct spelling: CapMyLead.


------------------------------
CLIENT CONTEXT
------------------------------

- Client was a travel agent.
- Existing WhatsApp-based CRM solutions felt too overwhelming.
- Client wanted a very simple solution.
- Goal was to create something their sales team could actually
  use and rely on.


------------------------------
PROBLEM STATEMENT
------------------------------

- Salespeople communicate with customers through WhatsApp
  and phone calls.
- Customers provide important information during calls/chats.
- Salespeople often do not record the information immediately.
- Entering information into Excel or another system during
  a call/chat is inconvenient.
- Later they may forget to enter it.
- As a result:
  - Customer information gets lost.
  - What the customer said may be forgotten.
  - Follow-up requirements may be missed.
  - Customer history is not properly structured.
- Follow-up process was also not properly organized.

Core problem:
- Important customer information and follow-up context
  was getting lost during the sales process.


------------------------------
SOLUTION
------------------------------

- I designed a simple CRM-like system around the existing
  behavior of the sales team.
- Built the application in Flutter.
- Initial project focused on the app side.

The application could:
- Capture notifications.
- Detect phone numbers from notifications.
- Check whether the number already exists in the database.

If number did not exist:
- Create a new contact.
- Store it in the contact database.
- Add it to the contact list.
- Generate a lead/contact identifier using a company prefix,
  lead information, and phone number.

Example:
- ZL100 + customer phone number.


------------------------------
POPUP / BUBBLE SYSTEM
------------------------------

- Built a popup/bubble interface that appears above the screen.
- Clicking the bubble opens a partial/half-screen interface.
- It does not completely cover WhatsApp.
- Salesperson can continue using WhatsApp while entering information.

The popup provides:
- Customer fields.
- Name.
- Other information.
- Notes.
- Recent contacts.

Recent contacts:
- Approximately 15 recent contacts shown.

Notes:
- Salespeople can record what the customer told them.


------------------------------
FOLLOW-UP SYSTEM
------------------------------

- Built a customizable follow-up system.
- Salespeople can create their own follow-up sequence.

Example:
- 7 touch points.
- 2-day gap between each touch point.

The system tracks:
- Which leads need follow-up.
- Which follow-up level/touch point they are currently at.

Morning workflow:
- Salesperson opens the app.
- Sees who needs follow-up.
- Sees what level they are at.

Action:
- Click one button.
- Open the relevant WhatsApp chat.
- Use the relevant data/message required for that follow-up.


------------------------------
PRODUCT THINKING
------------------------------

- Client specifically did NOT want an overwhelming CRM.
- Goal was simplicity.
- Solution was designed around the team's existing WhatsApp workflow.
- Instead of asking salespeople to separately maintain a CRM,
  the system captures information around their existing workflow.
- Popup reduces interruption.
- Information can be recorded while the customer conversation
  is happening.
- Follow-ups are structured without requiring a complicated CRM.


------------------------------
DISTRIBUTION / TECHNICAL DECISION
------------------------------

- Notification detection + popup/bubble functionality
  made Play Store distribution more complicated.
- I clearly communicated this limitation to the client.
- Client was comfortable with internal APK distribution.
- Sales team used Android phones.
- Application could be distributed internally as an APK.
- This avoided spending unnecessary time/money on Play Store
  approval/distribution during the initial phase.

Client preference:
- Avoid expensive official APIs.
- Avoid unnecessary external tools.
- Keep the system affordable.
- Keep the system under their internal control.

IMPORTANT:
- No official WhatsApp API was used.


------------------------------
TECH STACK
------------------------------

- Flutter
- Supabase
- Android notification detection
- Android popup/bubble functionality
- APK-based internal distribution
- No official WhatsApp API


------------------------------
AI CONTRIBUTION
------------------------------

AI TAG: 100%

- CapMyLead was completely built using AI-assisted development.
- Coding/implementation was done completely with AI.
- My contribution was:
  - Understanding the client's problem.
  - Engineering thinking.
  - Problem-solving.
  - Deciding what should be built.
  - Designing the solution/workflow.

ONE CHAT
============================================================

TYPE
- Personal project.
- Personal chatting application.


------------------------------
CORE IDEA
------------------------------

"One Chat, Many Languages."


------------------------------
PROBLEM STATEMENT
------------------------------

- The idea came from my experience living in Dubai.
- I was staying in a shared room/flat.
- Friends/people around me spoke different languages.
- Examples:
  - Marathi
  - Bengali
  - Iranian/Persian
  - Syrian/Arabic
  - Other languages
  - People from different countries and backgrounds

Problem:
- People may want to communicate with each other,
  but language becomes a barrier.
- A person wants to type in their own language.
- The other person wants to receive/read it in their own language.


------------------------------
PRODUCT IDEA
------------------------------

- One Chat allows each person to communicate in their own language.
- User selects their preferred language.
- User types in their own language.
- Recipient receives the message in their own language.

Example:

Person A:
Malayalam
       ↓
Person B:
English

or

Person A:
Bengali
       ↓
Person B:
Malayalam

Each person can communicate naturally in their own language.


------------------------------
AI TRANSLATION
------------------------------

- Google language translation is part of the concept.
- AI is used to make the translation more natural/context-aware.
- The goal is NOT just direct word-for-word/blender translation.
- AI should understand the context and create an optimized
  chat response in the recipient's language.

Goal:
- Preserve meaning.
- Make communication natural.
- Reduce language barriers.
- Allow people to speak/write naturally in their own language.


------------------------------
CORE PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY
------------------------------

- One chat interface.
- Multiple languages.
- Each person communicates in their own language.
- Translation happens in the background.
- The user does not need to manually translate messages.

============================================================
GENERAL PROBLEM-SOLVING STYLE
============================================================

Recurring characteristics in my work:

- Start with the actual user/business problem.
- Understand existing user behavior.
- Avoid unnecessary rebuilding.
- Prefer existing platforms/tools when they solve part of the problem.
- Build an MVP when validation is more important than completeness.
- Choose technology based on the problem.
- Focus on reducing friction for users.
- Think about the entire funnel/workflow rather than just the UI.
- Comfortable working across frontend and backend.
- Comfortable integrating third-party APIs/services.
- Comfortable with automation.
- Comfortable using AI as an implementation tool.
- Can independently take ownership of complete modules/products.
- Work iteratively with clients.
- Make practical trade-offs based on:
  - Cost
  - Speed
  - User behavior
  - Adoption
  - Technical limitations
  - Business requirements
